SMALL AI. VISIBLE REASONING.

No magic.
No silent guesses.

Pixel Witness uses symbolic AI: it searches 176 component-aware programs, keeps the ones that explain your examples, and chooses a question whose possible answers split those programs apart.

That is inductive program synthesis and active learning. It does not use an LLM, a pretrained network, or a remote service.

What the learner can express

One component selector followed by one action. Select by color, size, shape proportions, or canvas edge. Recolor, erase, outline, or move one pixel. Components are same-color pixels joined horizontally or vertically; touching different colors are separate.

What it cannot promise

Only 8 × 8 grids with three ink colors are supported. There is no semantic object recognition, multistep recipe, tolerance for mislabeled pixels, or proof that your intent lies in this language. Two different intentions may agree on every example here. A unique recipe is not certainty about your intent.

Precise editing behavior

Size selectors include every tied component. Outlines use four neighboring pixels, clip at edges, and never overwrite existing ink. Moving is atomic: the whole move stays unchanged if any selected pixel would leave the canvas or collide with unselected art.

Prior art and prototype status

Learning image edits and asking targeted questions are established ideas. This original prototype adapts them to a small, inspectable pixel-art workflow. See ImageEye, SmartLabel, and Pattern Analogies. Synthetic tests do not establish artist productivity or accuracy on real sprite collections.
